## Privacy, legal, and AI risk findings for the arrival kit

All 32 claims were checked against the cited sources. Thirty-one are confirmed and one ([23]) is partially confirmed. The corrections below tighten wording where the original went slightly beyond the source.

### Which law applies

The federal Personal Data Protection Law (PDPL), Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021, covers controllers and processors located in the UAE, and those abroad that process data of people inside the UAE [1]. It was issued on 20 September 2021 and came into force on 2 January 2022 [11]. It does not apply to free zone companies that are subject to their own data protection legislation, and a Chambers practice guide dated 10 March 2026 states that ADGM and DIFC entities are expressly excluded on that basis [2]. Therefore, a company licensed in ADGM follows the ADGM Data Protection Regulations 2021 (DPR 2021), while an onshore company follows the PDPL (this is our inference from [2]). The team is not yet incorporated, so the report should state both regimes and design to the stricter one.

The PDPL was only partly operational as of March 2026. The Chambers guide dated 10 March 2026 states that the Implementing Regulations had not been issued, that the Data Office had not become fully operational, and that enforcement activity was limited [12]. Breach reporting periods and transfer controls are deferred to those regulations [8], and controllers have six months after their issue to regularize, extendable by the Cabinet [11]. Gap: the status after March 2026 was not confirmed from an official source. Non-official pages suggest the regulations were still pending in September 2026 and that a Cabinet decision in June 2026 created a new federal authority absorbing the Emirates Data Office; neither point is verified, so the report should date this statement to March 2026.

### Core obligations that shape the design

- Consent is the default basis under the PDPL, with listed exceptions such as performing a contract with the data subject or protecting the data subject's interests [3]. Consent must be provable, clear, and easy to withdraw [4]. The UAE government portal summarizes the same rule [32].
- Data must be collected for a specific purpose, limited to that purpose, and not kept after the purpose is fulfilled unless anonymized [5].
- Sensitive personal data includes data revealing family, racial origin, political or philosophical opinions, religious beliefs, criminal records, biometric data, and health; biometric data expressly covers facial images that allow unique identification [6]. Passports and visa medical results can touch these categories, so the prototype should treat every uploaded document as sensitive (this is our conservative reading, not a regulator ruling).
- Transfers abroad are allowed, in cases approved by the UAE Data Office, to countries with data protection legislation or under international agreements; otherwise under a contract imposing PDPL requirements, the express consent of the data subject, or contract necessity [7]. A call to the OpenAI API from a default key is processing outside the UAE, so we treat it as such a transfer (our reading of the Article 1 definition of cross-border processing, not a regulator ruling).
- A data protection officer is triggered by high-risk new technologies, systematic assessment of sensitive data, or a large amount of sensitive data, and an impact assessment is required before high-risk processing of that kind [9]. People may object to automated decisions with legal or serious effect, subject to exceptions, and the controller must involve human review on request [10].
- Under ADGM rules, every ADGM registered entity that processes personal data registers as a data controller [13]. Special category data needs a section 5(1) lawful basis plus a section 7(2) condition such as explicit consent confirmed in words [14]. Breaches are notified to the Office of Data Protection without undue delay and, where feasible, not later than 72 hours after the controller becomes aware [15]. Fines reach $28 million, data subject requests are answered within 2 months, and high-risk processing requires an impact assessment in advance [16]. Minimization, storage limitation, and transfer rules follow the European model, because the DPR 2021 are closely based on the GDPR [17].

### OpenAI API data handling

API data is not used for training by default unless the customer opts in [18]. Abuse monitoring logs are generated for all API usage and kept for up to 30 days, unless longer retention is required by law [19]. The Responses API stores application state for 30 days by default unless `store` is false [20]. Zero Data Retention and Modified Abuse Monitoring require eligibility, prior approval from OpenAI, and acceptance of additional requirements [21], and images flagged as potential CSAM are retained for manual review even with Zero Data Retention [22]. OpenAI lists the UAE among its data residency regions, but residency outside the United States requires sales eligibility, approval for abuse monitoring controls, and a Modified Retention amendment, with a 10% price uplift on eligible newer models [23]. A hackathon key will not have UAE residency, so the prototype must not claim it. The honest pitch claim is therefore "our server stores no documents", not "nothing is retained anywhere" and not "data stays in the UAE".

### OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications 2025: what applies

The current list is the 2025 edition [24]. Four entries matter most:

- LLM01 Prompt Injection, including indirect injection from files and instructions hidden in images [25]. An uploaded "admission letter" can carry text aimed at the model.
- LLM02 Sensitive Information Disclosure, covering personal identifiers, health records, and legal documents [26].
- LLM09 Misinformation, including hallucination and user overreliance [27]. A wrong visa step is the most damaging failure for this product (our assessment).
- LLM10 Unbounded Consumption, relevant to a public demo URL with a paid key (our assessment; the entry name is from [24]).

### Buildable mitigations for a six-hour prototype

1. No server-side storage: send the image as a Base64 data URL through one serverless route, hold it in memory only, set `store: false`, and never log request bodies [20][31]. This does not remove OpenAI's own abuse monitoring logs, which are kept for up to 30 days [19].
2. Synthetic demo documents only, with a visible "sample document" watermark, plus a consent checkbox before any upload that names the transfer outside the UAE and the right to withdraw [4][7].
3. Injection defense: a system prompt that treats document text as data, a strict JSON schema for the extraction output, and no tools or actions available to the vision call [25].
4. Grounded answers: answer procedures only from a curated set of official pages, show the source link beside each step, label AI-generated content, and refuse with "verify with the official source" when nothing matches [27].
5. Input validation: accept PNG, JPEG, or WEBP only, cap file size and text length, and set `max_tokens` close to the expected response size [29][30][31].
6. Moderation: run the free `omni-moderation-latest` check on text and images before the main call; note that several categories (harassment, hate, illicit) are evaluated on text only [28].
7. Rate limiting: a per-IP or per-user limit on the route, and a hard spend limit set at the OpenAI project or organization level (the limit is not per key), which returns a 429 error once reached [30].
8. Disclaimer and a "report a problem" link on every screen; state that the tool is not legal advice and that vision accuracy drops on non-Latin, small, or rotated text [27][29][31].
