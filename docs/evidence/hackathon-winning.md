## What wins OpenAI-supported and Hub71 hackathons

### Sourced facts

OpenAI publishes a similar four-part rubric for its own online hackathons, and in both cases the four parts carry equal weight. OpenAI Build Week (registration from 9 July 2026, submissions 13 to 21 July 2026) scored Technological Implementation (how thoroughly and skillfully the project uses Codex), Design (a working or runnable project with a complete, coherent product experience), Potential Impact (a credible, specific case for solving a real problem), and Quality of the Idea (creativity and novelty) [1]. The OpenAI Open Model Hackathon (5 August to 11 September 2025) used Application of gpt-oss, Design, Potential Impact, and Novelty of the Idea, also equally weighted [3]; its Design criterion explicitly covers the safety of the user and a balanced blend of frontend and backend [4]. Both events capped the demo video at under three minutes and required a code repository and a written description of features and functionality [2][3].

OpenAI's community post announcing the Build Week winners (26 August 2026) states that the winning projects started with problems their builders knew well, citing a short-staffed veterinary clinic and speech accessibility among the examples [6]. A secondary write-up (note.com, not an OpenAI page) lists the eight winners across four tracks, including a veterinary triage app built by a veterinarian and a speech-support app for people with dysarthria [5]. This supports leading with Muhammet's first-hand experience as an international student and Recruitment Office staff member (inference).

Locally, the closest published rubric is the one-day NYU Abu Dhabi hackathon of 20 September 2026 (powered by JetBrains Academy), which placed 35% on product functionality, with the remaining criteria covering problem and insight, technical execution, and the final pitch; the weights of those three are not published [8]. Its winner was $ocial, an activity discovery and budgeting app [9]. The Cursor x eVoost AI hackathon at Hub71 (26 June 2026, Al Khatem Tower, 15th floor) supplied four challenge tracks, synthetic datasets, and a Next.js plus Tailwind starter kit; its Luma page gives no criteria, prizes, or winners [7]. The OpenAI Codex Hackathon in San Francisco (5 February 2026) was a one-day, in-person build with teams of up to four; its page also publishes no rubric [14]. A comparable lab-sponsored event, Cursor x Anthropic Hackathon Malaysia (6 to 7 December 2025), judged on Innovation, Vision and Delivery, and Refinement, without published weights [10]. The Hub71+ AI program page describes a 12-month, sector-agnostic program offering AED 250,000 in in-kind incentives plus AED 250,000 in cash through a SAFE note, and does not list OpenAI among its partners [15].

Demo guidance: Devpost's founder recommends three parts: set the scene by stating the problem in a few sentences; show the working project, which he calls the most important part (skip credential creation, keep needed text on the clipboard, briefly name key technologies or technical challenges, and show the problem being resolved); and close with one or two sentences on potential and ambitions [11]. Major League Hacking plans science-fair judging with two minutes for presentation and demo and one minute for questions and scoring, plus one minute of judge travel [12]. Devpost notes that a surprising number of entries are disqualified for missing baseline criteria and that, if a team cannot explain how to use its project in a sentence or two, the project may be too complicated [13]. Its judge panel stresses storytelling, double-checking every requirement, and a finished product a judge would want to use [16].
